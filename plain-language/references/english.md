# English principles

Apply these additions when writing, editing, reviewing, or translating into
English. Use the [shared principles](../SKILL.md)
throughout.

## 3xx Discourse

### E303 Let context carry what the reader already knows

When the context already tells the reader which one you mean, use the
bare noun, without a "that" clause or an "of" phrase. The bare noun names
the same concept, so it keeps the term consistent under
[P501](../SKILL.md#p501-keep-terms-consistent).

## 5xx Lexicon

### E501 Use everyday words

Use the word a native speaker would use when talking with a colleague,
such as "use" rather than "utilize" or "leverage", and "help" rather than
"facilitate". Keep technical terms and product names as
[P502](../SKILL.md#p502-use-the-terms-readers-in-the-field-use) asks.

### E502 Say what an idiom means

Write the plain meaning instead of an idiom whose words do not give it,
such as "table stakes", "move the needle", or "on the back burner".
Readers who learned English as a second language know the words but may
not know the idiom.
